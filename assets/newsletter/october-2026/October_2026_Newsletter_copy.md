A Spanish classic that only gets better with age.

Made from sheep’s raw milk in the La Mancha region of Spain and aged for 12 months, this Manchego is firm yet creamy, with buttery richness, toasted nut flavour, and a gentle caramel sweetness.

You’ll taste that unmistakable Manchego character: savoury, a little tangy, and wonderfully nutty. The texture makes it a natural for snacking, grazing boards, or a nibble before the turkey comes out.

Pair it with olives, toasted almonds, crusty bread, or a spoonful of quince paste for an easy Spanish-inspired board.

Twelve months of ageing offers plenty of Manchego character.

# **How to Host Thanksgiving Without the Stress**

\[Image: /assets/images/newsletter/2026/october/Autumn\_Charcuterie\_Gathering\_in\_a\_Cozy\_Kitchen.webp. Alt text: Relaxed Thanksgiving gathering with guests arriving and a small cheese and charcuterie board ready to enjoy\]

The secret to easier hosting? Don’t do everything at once.

A few small decisions made ahead of time can change the whole feel of the day.

Start with the appetizers. If dinner is an hour or two away, keep things simple. A small board with two or three cheeses, a little cured meat, crackers, olives, and a good preserve gives guests something delicious to enjoy without filling them up before the main event.

Pick at least one thing you can make completely the day before. Desserts, dips, cheese selections, and plenty of side dishes are happy to wait, which means fewer decisions on Thanksgiving Day.

Set out your serving dishes and utensils before anyone arrives. When you know where everything is going and what goes in it, the last-minute scramble mostly disappears.

And decide where you truly want to spend your time. Not every part of the meal has to be made from scratch.

A great Thanksgiving isn’t measured by how many dishes you made yourself. It’s measured by how much you enjoyed sitting down together.

# **What to Serve Before Thanksgiving Dinner**

\[Image: /assets/images/newsletter/2026/october/Rustic\_Autumn\_Charcuterie\_Board.webp. Alt text: Simple Thanksgiving appetizer board with two or three cheeses, charcuterie, olives, crackers, and preserves\]

The best Thanksgiving appetizer is a simple one. Two or three cheeses, a little charcuterie, something briny, a preserve, and good bread is all you need.

Choose cheeses with different textures and flavours, such as a creamy Brie, a nutty aged Manchego, and something with a little bite, rather than a giant selection. Add a small amount of charcuterie, a few olives, a preserve or condiment, and crackers or sliced baguette.

The goal is a little something to nibble on while everyone arrives, not a meal before the meal.

One easy trick: take your cheese out of the refrigerator 30 to 60 minutes before serving. Cheese is more aromatic, flavourful, and enjoyable closer to room temperature.

And resist the urge to keep refilling the board. Let it gradually disappear. Dinner is coming.

# **Thanksgiving Make-Ahead Timeline: What to Do and When**

\[Image.none\]

The easiest way to a calmer Thanksgiving is knowing what actually needs your attention at the last minute. Here’s a simple timeline to follow.

**The day before:** Prepare desserts, dips, dressings, sauces, and anything else that holds well overnight. Choose your cheeses and accompaniments, organize your serving dishes, and set the table if you can.

**Earlier in the day:** Prep vegetables, assemble dishes that only need reheating or baking, chill drinks, and get your appetizer ingredients ready.

**Just before guests arrive:** Finish anything that truly needs to be fresh, bring your cheeses toward room temperature, light the candles, and put out the first few nibbles.

Save the last hour for only the things that must happen then. It can completely change the pace of the day.

# **One Last Hosting Tip**

When everything is ready and the first guest arrives, stop preparing. There will always be one more thing that could be adjusted, garnished, warmed, rearranged, or added to the table.

At some point, the gathering matters more than the preparation so pour yourself something to drink, put out the cheese, and join everyone else. That’s what they came for.
